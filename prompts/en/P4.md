# P4 — Reverse test: did the concept solve a problem of its own time, or of a later one?

> Verbatim (source: `附录_数学思想史工具与指令集.md` §6). English rendering of the Chinese prompt.
> Use when: you suspect a concept has been back-dated.
> Grade note: the text uses the four-level A/S/U/F scheme; see the [spec](../../spec/evidence-tags.en.md).

```
Context: I am writing about how historical concepts were constructed, working backwards from today's questions. I suspect the concept {{concept}} has been back-dated.

Known:
- modern definition: {{...}}
- the earliest text where I think it appears: {{author, work, year, page}}

Answer in this order, tagging every step with a grade (A/S/U/F):

1. Split the modern definition into necessary conditions. For each: was it explicitly stated in {{year}}? Which condition was added later?
2. What specific difficulty did the author solve with it? Does that difficulty still exist today?
3. Reverse test: using only the tools available in {{year}}, could the difficulty have been solved another way? If yes, the concept was not a "necessary product" of its time.
4. Verdict: was the concept "born for a problem of its time" or "retro-attributed for a later problem"? Give your verdict, your confidence, and the kind of evidence that would overturn it.

Forbidden: arguing from "the internal logic of mathematical development".
```
