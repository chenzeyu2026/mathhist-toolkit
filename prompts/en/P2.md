# P2 — Evidence grading of a paragraph

> Verbatim (source: `附录_数学思想史工具与指令集.md` §6). English rendering of the Chinese prompt.
> Use when: a draft paragraph is finished; split it into claims and grade each one.
> Grade note: the text uses the four-level A/S/U/F scheme; see the [spec](../../spec/evidence-tags.en.md).

```
Below is a paragraph I wrote. Split it into sentences, grade every checkable claim, and say what would be needed to raise it.

Paragraph:
"""
{{your paragraph}}
"""

Grading scale:
A  primary source (with edition and page)
S  secondary (peer-reviewed monograph/paper, scholarly introduction, DSB)
U  unverified (a lead but no original seen)
F  popular (textbook, popular science, encyclopedia, lecture; lead only)

Second hand is not a grade. If you have only seen the material cited in a secondary work and not the
original page, keep the tag at S but name the intermediary in the locator, e.g.
"S｜secondary: as quoted in Grabiner 1981, p. 45". A may not be used for second-hand material.

Output a table: sentence | claim type (fact / causation / chronology / attributed intention) | current grade | basis | what is missing to reach A or S

Notes:
- "X thought of Y" is an attributed intention. It can reach S at most unless there is a first-person statement.
- "X caused Y" is causal and stays at S or F unless contemporary documents make the link directly.
- If one sentence mixes fact and causation, split it and grade the parts separately.
```
